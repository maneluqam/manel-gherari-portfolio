Research direction · 01

Joint 3C orchestration.

A unified view of communication, computing, and caching decisions for immersive applications operating across cloud, edge, and in-network resources.

The central question

How can a distributed infrastructure jointly decide where content should be cached, where computation should run, and how network capacity should be assigned—without violating stringent latency requirements?

01 · Motivation

Why joint decisions?

Immersive services couple network transport, rendering or processing, and content availability. Optimizing each resource independently can simply move the bottleneck elsewhere. My work therefore treats the three resource classes as one interdependent allocation problem.

02 · Formulation

System model

I model users, service components, content, network paths, and heterogeneous cloud–edge–INC nodes under capacity and end-to-end latency constraints. The resulting formulations expose the trade-offs among response time, resource use, and service acceptance.

03 · Method

Optimization approach

  • Mixed-integer nonlinear programming for a rigorous reference formulation.
  • A PSO–Greedy strategy for faster decisions at larger scales.
  • Comparative evaluation across user loads and distributed topologies.
04 · Contribution

What this adds

The contribution is an orchestration framework that exploits computing and caching opportunities inside the network rather than limiting placement to conventional cloud and edge servers.

Research outcome

The evaluation showed that coordinated 3C allocation can maintain mean latency around the requirements of immersive services while supporting high user acceptance. This work established the optimization foundation for my subsequent learning-based orchestration research.
