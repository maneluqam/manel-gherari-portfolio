Research direction · 04

From adaptive to responsible autonomy.

My current direction extends orchestration beyond latency: systems should anticipate change, explain trade-offs, remain stable, and account for their energy and infrastructure footprint.

The next question

How can autonomous orchestration systems make coordinated decisions across the cloud–edge–network continuum while remaining trustworthy, resource-efficient, and robust under real operational uncertainty?

01 · Context awareness

Sensing before acting

Future policies should combine workload, mobility, network quality, infrastructure state, and application context. The goal is to anticipate when action is useful instead of reacting to every short-lived change.

02 · Digital twins

Safer learning environments

I plan to investigate digital twins of distributed infrastructure as controlled environments for training, testing, and stress-testing orchestration policies before they influence operational systems.

03 · Agentic coordination

Structured autonomy

A promising direction is to combine specialized decision agents with explicit constraints, verification, and human oversight. Agentic AI should support orchestration reasoning without replacing safety boundaries or optimization guarantees.

04 · Sustainability

Beyond latency alone

Future objectives will include energy consumption, cooling and water implications, migration cost, hardware utilization, and service quality. The research challenge is to expose these trade-offs without making real-time control intractable.

Long-term research vision

I aim to build orchestration systems that connect mathematical rigor, learning, and programmable infrastructure: systems that decide at the right place and time, but also know when not to act. This agenda is intentionally cross-domain and can support immersive services, hospital operations, resilient public infrastructure, and other resource-constrained environments.
